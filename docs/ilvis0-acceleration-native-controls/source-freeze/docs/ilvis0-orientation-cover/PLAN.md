# A complete orientation cover: generated feasibility plan

The joint-affine support component passes its independent software controls.
The next experiment tests whether smaller native expansions improve the
nonlinear remainder without dropping any original orientation possibility.
This is a generated-data feasibility study, not an aircraft fit.

## Fixed comparison

Reuse the input definitions of the completed six-case native contraction:
three physical candidates, each under fixed-zero and profiled removal, 400
packets, the original 27-parameter bounds, known nonzero generated truth, six
training epochs at 0.20, 0.35, 0.60, 0.90, 1.20 and 1.50 seconds, and a
1.80-second prediction. Use the predeclared 1 mm training allowance and the
same 10 micrometre arithmetic allowance, which remains an assumption rather
than a global numerical certificate. Do not import aircraft measurements.

Partition normalized initial yaw [-1,1] into **eight closed dyadic sectors**.
Their union covers the entire original interval; keep every other parameter
bound unchanged. Recenter within each sector, form necessary training
inequalities with sector-wide remainders, and preserve their joint support.
Use a validated box contraction only as an additional necessary constraint.
Never discard a sector from an unverified LP infeasibility status.

Fix every center, training region and prediction bound before evaluating the
future truth. Generated training observations may be used; future truth must
not select sectors, widths, references or stopping rules. Retain every sector
not excluded by a checked necessary contradiction, including sectors that
contain no generated truth. Report the raw union, its nonlinear remainder
widths, and the saved broad-box baseline without hiding worse outcomes.

## Preparation and finite allowance

Before launch, implement and test exact coverage of the yaw interval, full-column
retention, arbitrary sector centers, training/future separation, all native
parameter/covariance identities and charge-before-evaluation behavior. Verify
that prior generated inputs and baseline hashes match. Freeze the complete
new implementation, generated inputs and numerical environment.

Proposed ceiling: **48 sector identities**, at most four native evaluations
and eight history bounds per identity: **192 native evaluations, 384 bounds
and 153,600 reserved packet steps**. Reserve all work before each operation.
At most 288 one-thread LP proposals and 576 exact support certificates are
permitted within those identities; each LP has a one-second cooperative limit.
One worker uses four numerical threads, with serial LP proposals limited to
one thread. Allow at most 600 cooperative seconds overall; the earlier measured
bound cost suggests roughly six minutes. No retries, extensions, signals or
reuse of another study's unused allowance.

Correctness requires the known generated parameters to survive their sector's
necessary constraints and every future truth to remain in the final union.
Every required case must complete. Report the width ratio for every axis and
case; a tenfold reduction everywhere is a component-improvement target, not
recording-scale predictive qualification. Correct but broad or incomplete
results block dependent observed predictions and remain published.

The study does not establish actual measurement errors, a global floating-point
certificate, long-record usefulness or absolute predictive adequacy. No observed
Earth-model fit or protected ESA evaluation follows automatically.
