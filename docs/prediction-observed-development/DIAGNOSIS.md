# Why the observed development pilot stopped

The one-recording ESA development pilot is **sealed as a technical failure**.
It used its full allowance of 3,000,000 native calls and stopped before the
second supplied error law's receiver-measurement bound was complete. The
80-billion packet/query allowance was not limiting: the saved prepayment
record shows 4.32 billion attempted packet visits. The task took 72.4 minutes.

Both law-specific training fits converged in **nine evaluations each**. Both
point forecasts and both physical-position bounds were saved. The first law
also saved its receiver-measurement bound. The second law did not. The worker's
withheld-data gate required both bounds before opening later receiver
positions, so **no withheld receiver score was produced**. These partial
artifacts are diagnostic evidence, not a passing two-law comparison.

The original task and its full charge remain unchanged. The next software
step is to qualify a **separately frozen continuation** that reads and verifies
these saved fits, forecasts and bounds, calculates only the missing
receiver-measurement bound under unchanged scientific inputs and method, and
opens the withheld positions only after both bounds are sealed. It must not
refit either law or relabel this failed study as a pass. A new finite allowance
and interruption-recovery check are required before that continuation runs.
