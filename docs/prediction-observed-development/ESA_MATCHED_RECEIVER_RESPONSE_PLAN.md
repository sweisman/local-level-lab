# Compare the receiver records under the same differentiation rule

Use the eight sealed 400-report windows, three receiver products and
both averaging widths. Keep the same center position, receiver kernel,
gravity cases and background-field cases used in the separate force
and direction scores.

Within each support, derive interval-average coordinate rates from
position differences and actual receiver times. The trapezoid relation
between nodal and interval-average rates leaves one alternating
component undetermined. Choose the minimum-norm nodal rates that
reproduce the position differences. Fit the same quadratic and an
alternating nuisance term to both nodal series at the primary receiver's
timestamps. Keep only the quadratic coefficients. Including the
alternating term preserves an exact quadratic velocity signal while
making the resulting fit independent of the unidentified component.
If positions are generated by trapezoidal integration of the
reported velocities, both methods must now predict the same forces.

Compare each force magnitude with the saved GIRAFE and raw iMAR
magnitudes after removing an earlier-only constant. Keep the
bandwidth-matched, gyro-assisted iMAR comparison separate. Compare
both predicted angle sets with the same saved iMAR pair angles,
with and without the antenna offset correction. Report the change
in the first window and retain every other window. The matched
method tests response equivalence under the declared discrete
integration. Differences can still arise from receiver errors
or within-interval motion.

Evaluate force vectors in batches and verify agreement with the
original scalar calculation. Freeze eight tasks, 12 operations
and 169,728 quadrature points per window, one thread and a
150-second limit. Use only the sealed inputs and saved sensor
measurements. No original recording, reserved group, model calibration
or decision threshold.
