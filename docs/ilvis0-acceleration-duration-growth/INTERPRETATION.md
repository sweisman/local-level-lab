# Duration growth is much smaller with the common acceleration preserved

All **48 identities pass** at 1.8, 3.6 and 7.2 seconds: every physical model,
processing case and original orientation sector. The generated inputs are
byte-identical to the previous duration screen. Native centers, all physical
directions and known parameter probes retain the original comparison limits.

The largest unshared width contributions are:

| Duration | North | East | Height | Previous construction, largest axis |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.8 seconds | 0.105 m | 0.131 m | 0.071 m | 15.42 m |
| 3.6 seconds | 0.355 m | 0.411 m | 0.231 m | 45.26 m |
| 7.2 seconds | 1.031 m | 1.164 m | 0.815 m | 150.23 m |

Preserving the common acceleration removes most of the earlier overestimate.
These numbers are **contributions to the mathematical allowance**, not complete
training-constrained prediction widths, measured aircraft errors, sensor drift
or calibrated intervals. They cannot be compared directly with the 1.8-second
complete union widths as though both tables measured the same quantity.

The study completed in 1,918 seconds, with 576 charged native calls and 144
bounds. Source/input/environment hashes and retained outputs are verified.
No archive originals, observed model fits or protected ESA outcomes were read.
The existing 53-stretch empirical result is unchanged.

The next scientific gate is a longer complete prediction comparison, including
the joint training constraints and every original orientation sector. The
[separate execution qualification](../native-sharded-execution/README.md)
prepares four workers with one thread each for this more expensive work.
One-thread native compatibility must pass before using that new allocation.
Recording-scale usefulness, numerical treatment and actual receiver-error
adequacy remain open.
